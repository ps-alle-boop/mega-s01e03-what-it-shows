0002 · Evidence before merging reports

owner Codex for ps-alle-boop proposed reviewed 2026-10-29 · 0 days ago

Context

The review of ticket #4471 shows why a shared symptom does not establish a shared defect. A subject line can describe several causes, while different subjects can describe the same cause. An intake tag is an initial guess, not a diagnosis.

Decision 0001 accepted merging on normalized subject alone. This proposed decision would supersede that rule when accepted. Decision 0001 remains unchanged as the record of the earlier decision.

Proposed decision

Merge reports only when evidence supports the same defect in the same product and component. Use a subject match to find reports worth comparing, then read their bodies and examine the relevant context and reproduction evidence before deciding.

For each comparison:

  1. Identify the product, affected component, tenant and path through the product.
  2. Record the expected behavior, observed behavior and steps or examples that reproduce it.
  3. Check the context that could distinguish causes, including tenant configuration, form version, locale and time zone where relevant.
  4. Merge only when the evidence supports the same causal mechanism and a shared fix. Keep distinct defects in separate issues, even when their subjects or visible symptoms match.
  5. Count each report ID once. Preserve its source and the evidence supporting its placement.

When the cause is unknown, keep the report separate and link any plausible related issues. Record the missing evidence and the next check needed. Matching subjects, matching intake tags, or two unknown classifications do not justify a merge. Revisit the placement when new evidence arrives.

Check the date reports against the live views

The date classifier in this export is specific to the booking paths under review in sched-prod. It uses the tenant’s locale, booking-form version and report channel to distinguish parser and form-hint failures. The reporter’s browser locale and the intake tag do not determine the tenant’s date conventions.

The explorer below makes that classifier’s distinctions visible. Changing a subject does not change its cause verdict. Matching subjects can still have different causes, and reports without a known cause remain separate.

The classifier is evidence about the paths it models, not a complete diagnosis of every report. If a report’s observed behavior conflicts with its assigned group, investigate the discrepancy and document it in the incident review. Do not turn a model limitation into a reason to discard the report or merge a distinct defect.

This date-specific classifier is not a universal rule for the desk’s other products. Apply the evidence standard above using the relevant product’s behavior and configuration.

Consequences and review

Triage requires reading reports and sometimes reproducing a problem before merging them. Engineering receives separate issues for demonstrated defects, with traceable report counts and explicit uncertainty where diagnosis is incomplete.

Review the written guidance against the live components and report evidence when the product or classifier changes. If this proposal is accepted, update the active triage guidance to point to it. Preserve accepted decision records; use a later record for a future replacement.
